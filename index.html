Module 1: Phishing & Social Engineering

Phishing

An attack that uses "harmless-looking" emails to trick you into revealing sensitve information,downloading malware, or sending money. Always check the address after the @ symbol, not the name you see.

Display Name vs Sender Address

Attackers often set the display name of an email to a trusted brand while using an unrelated or compromised domain

Domain TypoSquatting

Registering domains that look almost identical to legitimate ones, such as replacing letters with similar looking characters

Utilization of Urgency and Fear tactics

Messages are crafted to generate panic or extreme urgency to force quick, descisions on the victim removing the critical thinking aspect

Module 2: Smishing

Text messages are sent with malicous links claiming to be alerts such as package delivery alerts, bank security warnings, etc.

Vishing

Phone calls where a scammer impersonates trusted authorities to extract personal information.

Deceptive Links & Attachments

Url masking

Using URL shorteners or hyperlinked text to disguise the actual destination URL

Dangerous File Extensions

Hiding executable code in double extensions ot telying on archive files to bypass standard email filters

Spear Phishing

Customized emails targeting a specific person or small team using personal details that is collected from public records or social media.

Module 2: Passwords & Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Passwords vs Passphrases

Traditional password requirements oftern push users toward hard to remember strings, while passphrases provide greater "randomness" while remaining easy to remember.

Password Entropy

A measure of how unpredictable a password is. Length expands search space exponentialy compared to short password with special characters.

Dictionary Attacks & Brute-Forcing

Automated tools that test millions of common words, variations and letter/number combinations per second

Password Managers

Credential Stuffing Threat

When one site suffers a data breach, automated bots try those leaked username/passwords across hundreds of services

Built in Generators & Auto-fill

Creating strong, random strings for every site without needing to memorize them manually

Types of Multi-Factor Authentication(MFA)

MFA requires two or more distinct verification factors before granting accesss. Something you know, something you have, or something you are

SMS/Text Based Authentication

Codes sent via text message which are vulnerable to sim swapping attacks

Authenticator Apps

Time based one time passwords generated locally by apps like google authenticator using an encrypted shared secret

Module 5: Safe Browsing Practices

Why Open Networks are Risky

On an unecnrypted public network, traffic between your device adn the accesspoint can be captured by anyone else on that network.

What you Give Away

Login credentials, session cookies, and unencrypted form data are the common targets. Sites using HTTPS protect the content of a page, but not which sites you visit.

Staying Safe

Use a VPN

A VPN encrypts your traffic end to end, so a network eavesdropper sees only ciphertext.

Verify the Network Name

Ask staff for the exact SSID rather than connecting to the first matching name.

Prefer HTTPS and avoid Sensitive Logins

Skip banking, shopping, and anything requiring payment details while on an open network. Wait until you are on mobile data or a trusted connection.

Lesson 6: Device & Software Maintenance

Why Updates Matter

Most malware relies on vulnerabilities that vendors have already patched. Running outdated software means running known holes.

Patching Your Operating System

Enable automatic updates for your OS and browser. These two get the most attention from attackers because they run continuously and handle untrusted content.

Application Updates

Third-Party Software

PDF readers, media players, and office suites are common targets. Update them alongside your OS rather than once a year.

Remove What You Don't Use

Every installed program is potential attack surface. Uninstall anything you no longer need.

Antivirus & Firewalls

Keep built-in protection enabled and its definitions current. It won't catch everything, but it catches the known and the lazy.

Backups

The 3-2-1 Rule

Three copies of your data, on two different media, with one stored off-site. This is what makes ransomware survivable.

Test Your Restores

A backup you have never restored from is an assumption, not a backup. Recover a file periodically to confirm it works.
